# Reading the age chart

Figure: `figures/wealth_gap_by_age.png`. Each column is an age group of the
household head.

- **Top row:** median net worth for the two education groups. Each panel has
  its own scale, so compare the two lines within a panel, not heights across
  panels.
- **Bottom row:** the college-to-high-school-or-less ratio. These panels share
  one scale, and the dashed line is the all-ages ratio for reference.
- Shaded areas are 95% confidence intervals.

## What to point out

- **Ages 55–64 are the clearest case:** the ratio went from 3.3× in 1989 to
  11.4× in 2025. College families' median roughly doubled, from $557k to
  $1.19M, while the high-school-or-less median fell from $171k to $105k. Even
  the lower end of the 2025 range (7.3×) is above 1989's upper end (6.3×).
- **Ages 45–54 widened too,** from 3.8× to 7.1×, though the ranges overlap more.
- **Under 35, 35–44 and 75+ show no clear widening.** Under 35 ends at 4.0×
  against 5.8× in 1989, and 35–44 is 4.1× at both ends.
- **The bands are wide in every panel,** because each holds only about 60–650
  sampled families. Single-year spikes, like 35–44 jumping to 11× in 2013 and
  back to 4.1× in 2025, are mostly noise.

## One-line summary

"The overall widening isn't just because college graduates are now older. It
shows up within middle age, and most strongly as people near retirement."
